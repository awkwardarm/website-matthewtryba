PROs, Copyrights & Royalty Splits — FAQ for Artists

How Performing Rights Organizations (PROs) work, how publishing and master splits are structured, and how copyright registration applies to your releases.

1

What is a PRO, and why do I need to join one?

A Performing Rights Organization (PRO) — such as BMI or ASCAP in the United States — collects performance royalties generated whenever your music is broadcast publicly. This includes television broadcasts, radio plays, live venue performances, and digital streams on services like Spotify and Apple Music.

  • Joining a PRO is free or low-cost.
  • Every songwriter on a project must be registered with a PRO so royalties can be accurately routed.
2

How do performance splits work (The 100% vs. 200% System)?

Different PROs calculate total songwriting shares using different percentage scales, though the payout math ends up identical:

  • ASCAP (100% System): Calculates total composition as 50% Writer Share + 50% Publisher Share = 100% Total.
  • BMI (200% System): Calculates total composition as 100% Writer Share + 100% Publisher Share = 200% Total.
  • Self-Published Artists: If you do not have a formal publishing deal, you do not need to register as a publisher. 
3

What is the difference between Composition Rights and Master Rights?

Every recorded song contains two distinct legal copyrights:

  • The Composition (Publishing): The underlying song itself — the lyrics, melodies, chord progressions, and musical arrangements. Split among the songwriters and producers based on creative contribution.
  • The Master (Sound Recording): The specific physical/digital audio recording of that composition. Split among the project's financiers, artists, and producers per the master agreement.
4

What is an IPI / CAE number?

An IPI (Interested Parties Information) or CAE number is a unique 9-digit identification number assigned to you by your PRO once your writer account is approved. It identifies you globally across all performance rights databases. You must provide your IPI number on all collaboration split sheets and registration documents.

5

Do I need to formally register my song with the US Copyright Office before release?

  • Inherent Copyright: Under US law, copyright protection is automatic the moment your lyrics, melody, or audio are fixed in a tangible medium (written on paper, saved in a DAW, or emailed). Digital timestamps (emails, DSP uploads) establish a clear paper trail of ownership.
  • Formal Registration: Registering with the US Copyright Office (copyright.gov) provides formal legal proof required if you ever need to file a copyright infringement lawsuit in federal court.
  • Batch Strategy: Registering individual singles ($45–$65 per song) gets expensive. The most cost-effective approach is to batch 5 to 10+ songs together and register them all at once as an album or collection of works under a single registration fee.
