Sync Licensing & Pitching — FAQ for Artists

How synchronization licensing works for songs produced with TRYBA MUSIC, LLC, how representation through my licensing agent works, and what to expect during the pitching process.

1

What is the primary role of a music licensing agent?

A licensing agent bridges the gap between your music and music supervisors working on TV shows, films, trailers, commercials, and video games. They maintain direct relationships with supervisors, pitch relevant tracks when briefs come in, negotiate fees, draft contracts, and collect sync revenues.

2

How does sync representation work with TRYBA MUSIC?

When we produce a song that meets commercial sync standards and we have all split paperwork signed, I can submit the track to my exclusive licensing agent in Los Angeles (PEN Music Group).

  • Filter System: The agent trusts me as a curator. If I submit a track, they add it to their catalog for active pitching.
  • Exclusivity: Representation is exclusive for sync for a standard 2-year term. You cannot sign the same track with another sync agent or library during this window.
  • Label Deals: Exclusive sync representation does not restrict you from signing a record deal with a label later. If a label deal happens, we address it then.
3

How are sync fees and splits structured?

  • Agent Commission: The licensing agent takes a 30% commission on any sync fee they generate.
  • Remaining Net (70%): The remaining 70% is distributed to the master owners and songwriters based on the agreed splits in our signed Collaboration & Master Agreement.
  • Example: On a $1,000 TV placement:
Licensing Agent fee (30%)$300
Remaining balance$700
50/50 master & 50/50 composition split — each receive$350
4

What is a typical sync fee for an independent artist?

Sync fees for unknown or independent artists generally range between $500 and $10,000 per placement depending on the media type (background TV, major network, national ad, or trailer). High-profile placements or film trailers can yield significantly higher fees (e.g., $30,000+).

5

Will the licensing agent keep me updated every time my song is pitched?

No. Sync agents manage thousands of tracks and pitch at high volume. You will not receive updates when a track is pitched or included on a playlist sent to a supervisor. You will only hear from us when there is a concrete development, such as a music supervisor placing a hold on a track, an "Approval Required" email confirming placement terms and pricing, or a confirmed sync.

6

How long does it take for a track to get placed and paid?

  • Placement Timeline: Sync is unpredictable. A track might land a placement in one month, or it might take five years. Think of it as placing a fishing lure in the water.
  • Payout Timeline: Getting paid takes time. Between a scene airing on television and the licensing agent executing accounting and distributing funds, it typically takes 9 to 12 months to receive payment.
7

What does "One-Stop" mean, and why is paperwork required in advance?

"One-Stop" means all rights holders (for both the composition/publishing and the master recording) are accounted for, agreed upon, and can be cleared quickly through a single point of contact.

  • Music supervisors work under extreme time pressure. If paperwork is incomplete or splits are unconfirmed, they will skip the track.
  • Prerequisite: Before any song is pitched, our Collaboration & Master Agreement must be fully signed, and all writers must be registered with their respective PROs.
