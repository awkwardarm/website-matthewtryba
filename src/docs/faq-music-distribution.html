Music Distribution & Master Audio Formats — FAQ for Artists

Audio file deliverables, distribution platforms, profile hygiene, and marketing tactics for releasing independent music.

1

What master files do I receive upon completing a project?

Once final invoice payment is settled, you receive a complete deliverable archive containing:

  • Full Master (WAV): The high-resolution, uncompressed master audio file used for digital distribution.
  • Reference MP3: A compressed audio file suitable for quick email sharing or mobile listening.
  • Instrumental Mix: The full track mix with all lead and backing vocals muted.
  • TV Mix: The full track with lead vocals muted, but retaining backing vocals/harmonies (used for television performances or sync needs).
  • Acapella: Isolated vocal tracks.
  • Stems: Individual stem groups (Drums, Bass, Synths, Guitars, Vocals) for remixing or trailer customization.
2

What is the difference between a WAV and an MP3 file?

  • WAV: Uncompressed, full-resolution audio (CD/lossless quality). This is the only file format you should submit to digital distributors (DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby, etc.).
  • MP3: A compressed audio file with a significantly smaller file size. High-bitrate MP3s (320 kbps) sound virtually identical to WAVs on consumer headphones, but the compression strips data that DSPs (Digital Service Providers) require for encoding.

Which distributor should I use to release my music?

All major independent distributors (DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby, Ditto) serve the same primary function: delivering your audio to Spotify, Apple Music, Tidal, Amazon, and TikTok.

4

Can I change my artist name after releasing music?

Avoid changing your artist name after distribution whenever possible.

  • Changing an artist name across DSPs is a slow, error-prone process.
  • On most platforms, changing your name requires pulling the original track down and re-uploading it. This permanently destroys your existing stream counts, playlist placements, user saves, and algorithmic momentum.
  • Choose an artist name you are willing to commit to long-term before submitting your first release.
5

How do I promote my song once it is distributed?

  • Spotify for Artists Pitching: Submit your unreleased WAV file to Spotify editors through your free Spotify for Artists dashboard at least 2 to 3 weeks prior to the release date. This is the only legitimate, free way to be considered for editorial playlists.
  • Avoid Paid Playlist Scams: Never pay third-party companies or services promising guaranteed playlist placement. These services frequently use bot farms. Spotify actively detects fake streams and will remove your song or ban your artist profile entirely.
  • Organic Content & Ads: Sustainable growth comes from building a consistent brand on short-form video platforms (TikTok, IG Reels, YouTube Shorts).
